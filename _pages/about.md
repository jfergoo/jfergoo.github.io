I am an <b>applied mathematician</b> working on the dynamics of complex natural systems. I use <b>dynamical systems</b>, <b>network theory</b>, and <b>numerical simulations</b> to understand how structure shapes collective behaviour — across substrates including ice (glacier and permafrost dynamics) and brains (cortical microcircuits and connectome-driven neural activity). My recurring interest is in problems where directed transport, feedback, and parameter inference meet — channelization in subglacial drainage, feed-forward amplification in cortex, and inverse problems for natural-system parameters from sparse observations.

I obtained my PhD at the [University of Zürich](https://www.uzh.ch/) under Andreas Vieli, working on mathematical models of debris-covered glaciers. I then moved to the [Institute of Science and Technology Austria (ISTA)](https://ista.ac.at/) as a postdoc in theoretical and computational neuroscience, where I studied how network topology and brain-wide signalling shape neuronal dynamics. More recently, I have been a visiting scientist at the [Medical University of Vienna](https://www.meduniwien.ac.at/) and a visiting lecturer at the [African Institute for Mathematical Sciences (AIMS)](https://aims.ac.za), South Africa, where I co-taught a graduate course in Neuro AI.

Currently based in Vienna, Austria.
